# How thresholds are calibrated in this system

Date: 2026-09-26

This system makes two kinds of automated judgement against a numeric cutoff:
whether a window of teacher speech is on-topic, and whether a user was visually
attentive. Both cutoffs started life as hand-picked constants. Both have since
been replaced by fitted values, by the same method, recorded in two places:

| Threshold family | Values | Fitted in |
|---|---|---|
| Semantic cosine `UPPER` / `LOWER` | `0.36` / `0.14` | [`apps/semantic_analysis/ground_truth/design_decisions.md`](../apps/semantic_analysis/ground_truth/design_decisions.md) (D1–D3), [`threshold_experiment_findings.md`](../apps/semantic_analysis/ground_truth/threshold_experiment_findings.md) |
| Video attention threshold + sub-signal weights | `0.6123`; `(0.55, 0.40, 0.05)` | [`docs/video_engagement/design-choices.md`](./video_engagement/design-choices.md) |
| Engagement fusion weights | `a=3.4486188047224937`, `b=3.9024996131627105`, `bias=-2.4287558170454084` | [`apps/warning_engine/ground_truth/fit_fusion_weights.py`](../apps/warning_engine/ground_truth/fit_fusion_weights.py), `fusion_corpus.csv` |

This report describes the shared method, then what is specific to each, then
what remains uncalibrated. It is a summary of those documents, not a
replacement — the per-row results live in
`threshold_fixture_results.json` and `weight-search-results.json`.

---

## The shared method

Five steps recur in both calibrations. They were arrived at independently, a
week apart, which is part of why they are worth writing down as a house style.

**1. Ground truth first, with a written rubric.** Neither calibration fits
against model output. The semantic work has
[`labeling_rubric.md`](../apps/semantic_analysis/ground_truth/labeling_rubric.md)
defining four labels and their tie-breaks; the video work defines five labels
and a pan/tilt-to-label rubric. Both rubrics were written *before* the fit, and
both explicitly **exclude an ambiguous band** rather than push it into whichever
class is convenient — the video fit drops 480 frames in the 15°–45° head-turn
range, because a 30° turn could equally be a glance at a second monitor.

The video work also rejected an otherwise usable public dataset on exactly this
principle: `cnn-based-drowsiness-detection-data` is MIT-licensed and fetchable,
but its labels were themselves generated by a MediaPipe EAR heuristic. Fitting
our MediaPipe EAR signal against it would have measured agreement with another
heuristic rather than with reality.

**2. Grid search, in pure Python.** The semantic fit sweeps cutoffs on a 0.01
grid across the observed cosine range. The video fit sweeps the 231-triple
weight simplex at step 0.05 and then sweeps threshold candidates. Neither adds
a dependency: ROC-AUC, balanced accuracy and threshold selection are
implemented by hand in `weight_search.py`, and the semantic experiment runs on
the standard library alone.

**3. Hold out by group, never by row.** Both documents treat a random row split
as invalid. The video holdout reserves 4 of 15 head-pose **subjects** and 4
eye-state **shards**, because consecutive frames of one person are
near-duplicates and a frame-level split would report memorisation. The semantic
experiment runs leave-one-example-out *and* leave-one-topic-out, and its
instructions for real data are emphatic: assign source sessions to calibration
or evaluation **before** inspecting results, keep variants of a session in its
original split, and never manufacture independent sessions by concatenating
fixture sentences.

**4. The accuracy optimum does not automatically win.** This is the most
distinctive shared decision, and both documents reach it from different
directions:

- Semantic: plain accuracy ties across a whole plateau of `LOWER` values, but
  the *error mix* does not. `0.17` is the accuracy optimum; `0.14` was adopted.
- Video: plain accuracy is rejected outright as the objective, because on an
  unbalanced corpus it can be won by always predicting the larger class.
  Ranking is by mean per-split ROC-AUC, then minimum per-split balanced
  accuracy, then class separation. Pearson *r* is computed and reported but
  deliberately not optimised — against binary targets it rewards a spread-out
  distribution rather than a separable one.

Tie-break rules were fixed before running in both cases, and both are explicit
that a tie should not be awarded to whatever the sort happened to put first.

**5. The fitted number lives where the code reads it, and is pinned.** The
video weights and threshold are constants in `scoring.py`, overridable by
`VIDEO_WEIGHT_*` env vars whose defaults are set in `docker-compose.yml`; two
tests pin them to the recorded `weight-search-results.json`, so a weight cannot
drift away from the experiment that justified it. Both documents state the
exact command to re-run the fit.

## Semantic cosine thresholds: `0.36 / 0.14`

**Data.** 60 synthetic single sentences across six topics (24 `correct`, 18
`weakly_correct`, 18 `incorrect`), recorded by input SHA-256. Cosine scores are
reused from the frozen stock-SBERT fixture pipeline — no encoder
recomputation, no relabeling from model predictions.

**Why the old constants were so wrong.** `0.55 / 0.35` were never fitted. On
this fixture the `incorrect` class tops out at cosine `0.164` while on-topic
content bottoms out at `0.148` — the entire decision region sits below `0.25`.
A `LOWER` of `0.35` therefore labelled 40% of genuinely on-topic windows as
off-topic.

| Thresholds | Correct | Macro-F1 | False off-topic |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `0.55 / 0.35` (old) | 28/60 | 0.427 | 40.5% |
| Calibrated global `0.36 / 0.14` | 45/60 | 0.744 | 7.1% |
| Per-contract `UPPER` | 42/60 | 0.691 | 7.1% |

`UPPER = 0.36` is the grid optimum and is stable: identical in all 60
leave-one-example-out folds and all 6 leave-one-topic-out folds.

**The asymmetric-cost decision (D2).** `LOWER = 0.17` catches 18/18 off-topic
examples at the cost of 2 false accusations; `0.14` catches 17/18 with 0. The
document adopts `0.14` and argues the asymmetry is structural rather than a
preference: a false `incorrect` is the only classification that can escalate a
warning, and warnings feed trust scores and escrow outcomes, so it penalises
someone who was doing the work. A missed `incorrect` costs one window of
evidence, and escalation requires *consecutive* off-topic windows anyway. It
also names the alternative as a product call: set `0.17` for maximum recall and
record the choice.

**Per-topic thresholds were tested and rejected (D3).** 42/60 against 45/60;
they changed three predictions from right to wrong and gained none. Under
leave-one-topic-out they are mathematically identical to global calibration,
since every unseen topic falls back to the global pair. The family-calibration
code is kept and tested but stays unused for want of per-group support.

**Stated limits.** 60 synthetic single sentences, six topics, one author, while
production classifies ~25-second multi-sentence windows. The document is
explicit that this justifies *replacing two unfitted constants* and does not
establish production accuracy.

## Video attention threshold: `0.6123`

**Data**, both licence-verified on the download date, with URL, byte count and
SHA-256 recorded: the Gourier/Crowley Head Pose Image Database (2,820 real
photographs, pan/tilt encoded per filename) for `head_deviation`, and 1,100
balanced frames from `MichalMlodawski/closed-open-eyes` (ODC-BY) for
`eyes_open`. 3,440 manifest frames → 3,272 with a detected face → 2,295 fit /
977 held out.

**Labels are designed one-per-sub-signal.** `eyes_closed` breaks `eyes_open`
alone, `looking_away` breaks `head_deviation` alone, `gaze_off` breaks
`gaze_centered` alone. The document explains why this structure is necessary
rather than tidy: without it the three weights are **not identifiable**, since a
corpus of plain "attentive vs not" fits every weighting equally well.

`absent` frames are deliberately *not* scored 0.0. Scoring needs landmarks, a
faceless frame yields none, and production already skips no-detection frames —
so scoring them zero would fit a case the service never evaluates. They measure
detector coverage instead.

**How the threshold itself is picked.** Predict attentive when
`score >= threshold`. Candidates are the midpoints between adjacent distinct
scores, plus one below the minimum and one above the maximum, ranked by
**Youden's J** (`tpr - fpr`, equivalently balanced accuracy). Ties take the
**median** tied candidate, so the threshold sits in the middle of the winning
plateau rather than hugging an observed score.

**Results.** Fit set mean per-split AUC 0.9894, balanced accuracy 0.9569. The
held-out set — the only numbers not optimised for — gives AUC **0.9886**,
balanced accuracy **0.9617**, tpr 0.975, fpr 0.052. Mean fused score runs
`attentive` 0.870, `looking_away` 0.530, `eyes_closed` 0.335. The previous
placeholder `(0.4, 0.4, 0.2)` ranks 65th of 231.

**Degenerate baselines as a sanity check.** Each single-signal weighting
collapses on the split it cannot see — eyes-only scores AUC 0.4773 on the
head-pose split, *below chance*. That is the evidence the grid is measuring
something real rather than ranking noise.

**The plateau and the `w_gaze` floor.** 29 of 231 triples land within 0.005
mean-AUC of the best, so the choice inside the plateau is made explicitly:
keep `w_gaze >= 0.05`, then maximise minimum per-split balanced accuracy, then
minimise `w_gaze`, then maximise separation. The floor and the "minimise"
rule pull against each other on purpose. `gaze_centered` is the one sub-signal
no fetched dataset can rank, so it gets the least weight the evidence permits —
but exactly zero would *delete* the signal from production, a stronger claim
than "unmeasured". The floor costs 0.0004 mean AUC.

## Engagement fusion weights: sigmoid-logistic model

**From fixed heuristic to fitted function.** The warning engine previously blended
speech and video engagement with fixed constants: `ENGAGEMENT_FUSION_W_SPEECH=0.7`
and `ENGAGEMENT_FUSION_W_VIDEO=0.3`. This section replaces those constants with
a fitted sigmoid-logistic model, trained on synthesized paired speech and video
engagement data.

**The model.** A logistic function of the form `sigmoid(a * speech_score + b * video_score + bias)`,
where `a`, `b`, and `bias` are the fitted coefficients. The model produces a probability
in [0, 1] that a fusion window is **engaged** (label field `engaged_label`, logged as
`fused_engagement_score`), fusing the raw engagement scores from `semantic_analysis` (speech)
and `video_engagement` (video) without requiring manual weight tuning. This is an engagement
classifier, not a topic-adherence classifier — topic adherence is a separate axis owned by
`semantic_analysis`'s cosine-threshold classification (see above); engagement fusion only
blends speech engagement with video attention.

**Data and fit.** Fitted on 200 synthetic scenarios generated by
`apps/warning_engine/ground_truth/synthesize_fusion_corpus.py`, one row per `scenario_id`,
with latent engagement levels `{high: 0.85, medium: 0.5, low: 0.15}` and independent
Gaussian noise per modality (sigma=0.1), plus ~20% deliberate speech/video disagreement cases.
Binary labels are derived: `engaged_label = 1` if the average true engagement ≥ 0.5, else 0.
Split by `scenario_id` at a 0.2 holdout fraction yields 160 training rows / 40 test rows.
Because the corpus has exactly one row per scenario (200 scenarios = 200 rows), splitting
"by scenario, never by row" is equivalent on this corpus to an ordinary row-level split —
the scenario-grouping code in `fit_fusion_weights.py` is correct and would matter for a
corpus with multiple rows per scenario, but it provides no extra leak protection here, since
there is nothing to leak between rows of the same scenario when each scenario has only one row.
The script `apps/warning_engine/ground_truth/fit_fusion_weights.py` fits logistic regression
to the training split via gradient descent.

**Results on the held-out split.**
| Metric | Fitted sigmoid-logistic | Fixed `0.7/0.3` baseline |
|---|---:|---:|
| AUC | **0.997151** | 0.980057 |
| Accuracy | **0.950000** | 0.850000 |

The fitted model improves both AUC (by 1.7 percentage points) and accuracy (by 10 percentage points) over the fixed baseline on the
same held-out split.

**Stated limits.** The entire fit is on synthesized data: `fusion_corpus.csv` is generated
by `apps/warning_engine/ground_truth/synthesize_fusion_corpus.py`, pairing synthetic
speech scores with simulated video attention. No real speech+video session data exists yet, so
this fit cannot validate against actual user behaviour. A self-training extension
(`apps/warning_engine/ground_truth/semi_supervised_fusion_fit.py`) was built to improve the model with
unlabeled data, validated only on a synthetic unlabeled stand-in (`unlabeled_stand_in.csv`),
not yet run against real session logs. The fitted coefficients are a starting point; production
use should gather paired engagement traces from live sessions and refit.

**Known caveat: `fused_engagement_score` scale differs by code path.** In `warning_engine/main.py`,
the two-score fusion path (both speech and video present) now runs the sigmoid-logistic model
above and logs its `[0, 1]` probability output as `fused_engagement_score`. The single-score
fallback path (only speech, no video — unchanged by this fit) still logs the raw speech score
directly, with no sigmoid applied. The two regimes are therefore not on a comparable scale: for
example, speech=0.6/video=0.8 through the two-score path logs a `fused_engagement_score` around
0.94, while a lone speech=0.6 through the fallback path logs 0.6 directly. This is a known,
undecided inconsistency — unifying the two paths' output scale is a future design decision, not
addressed here.

## What is not calibrated

The documents are as clear about this as about what was fitted.

1. **The fusion weights are fitted on synthetic data only.** The sigmoid-logistic
   model (see "Engagement fusion weights" above) replaces the old `0.7`/`0.3` heuristic
   with fitted coefficients, but is trained entirely on synthesized speech and video pairs.
   No paired real speech+video session data exists yet, so the fit cannot validate against
   actual user behaviour. The coefficients are a starting point; production use should
   gather engagement traces from live sessions and refit.
2. **`gaze_centered` has no ground truth.** Columbia Gaze is the only candidate
   with head-forward/eyes-aside annotations and its download is gated behind an
   email form. There are no `gaze_off` frames in the corpus at all. There is
   also evidence the corpus *cannot* isolate gaze: a pure-gaze weighting still
   scores AUC 0.9207 on the head-pose split, because the eye-corner landmarks
   compress as the head turns.
3. **Domain gap is the headline caveat on the video threshold.** The head-pose
   imagery is studio-lit against a neutral background and the eye-state split is
   AI-generated, so `0.6123` is a starting point validated on public data, not
   on this rig's webcam, lighting, framing or compression.
   `collect_attention_frames.py` exists to close that with a short capture.
4. **The two video splits are not independent evidence for one threshold.** Each
   speaks to one sub-signal, so the pooled threshold is a compromise between
   two distributions that would never co-occur in a single session.
5. **The video threshold currently drives no decision.** `video_engagement`
   posts a continuous score; nothing thresholds it. `ATTENTIVE_SCORE_THRESHOLD`
   is the constant to read wherever a boolean "was this user attentive" is
   needed — a per-window flag, an attentive-fraction in the verdict, or a
   video-side escalation rule mirroring the consecutive-off-topic logic.
6. **The semantic thresholds want refitting on real windows.** The fixture is
   synthetic single sentences; production classifies ~25-second windows. The
   export path and the split discipline for doing this are already written down.
7. **`weight_search.py`'s backend-correlation mode has never run**, because
   `video_engagement_results` and `engagement_score_log` are empty. It is also
   the weaker experiment, since the speech engagement score is a proxy rather
   than a label of visual attention.
8. **The cosine gate does not encode polarity.** "I will pay you" and "I will
   not pay you" share nearly all their words, and cosine similarity does not
   encode polarity, so an on-topic reversal can pass the similarity threshold
   (not measured here). The lexicon-based reversal check
   (`apps/semantic_analysis/reversal_detection.py`) partially covers this; on a
   small synthetic corpus it scores precision 0.41 / recall 0.65 and fails on
   double negation. See
   [reversal_detection_findings.md](../apps/semantic_analysis/ground_truth/reversal_detection_findings.md).

Separately, and not a calibration issue: the weights were fitted on MediaPipe
sub-signals only. The Rekognition path derives `gaze_centered` from yaw alone,
so the same weights are applied to a coarser signal there — and `aws` scores
one mid-window frame where `local` averages the whole window.

## Reproducing either fit

```bash
# Semantic — standard library only, from the repo root
python3 apps/semantic_analysis/ground_truth/threshold_experiment.py \
  --fixture-diagnostic apps/semantic_analysis/ground_truth/synthetic_dataset.csv \
  --output apps/semantic_analysis/ground_truth/threshold_fixture_results.json

# Video — from apps/video_engagement, after fetching the two corpora
#          (full curl/tar/shard loop in design-choices.md)
venv/bin/python weight_search.py \
  --corpus datasets/head_pose_image_database datasets/closed_open_eyes \
  --step 0.05 --top 12
```

Neither corpus is committed: the datasets are gitignored, and captured webcam
frames are personal data that must never reach the repo.
